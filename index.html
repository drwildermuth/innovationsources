Who is Harborline Outfitters?

▶ If the video above doesn't load, watch it on YouTube

Harborline Outfitters has sold hiking, camping, and paddling gear out of the same three coastal counties for over thirty years. Twelve stores, family-owned, staffed by people who actually know the trails they're selling gear for.

That reputation isn't enough anymore. Foot traffic is down for the second straight quarter. National chains undercut Harborline on price; online retailers undercut it on convenience. Last month, a longtime regional competitor closed its last two stores — a warning sign, not a relief.

The board approved a modest innovation fund this year, mostly as a pilot: prove that a real recommendation, built the right way, can move the needle before committing more. If this quarter's pick lands, the fund likely grows next year. If it lands flat — too safe to matter, or too reckless to trust — the board may fold the innovation budget back into standard operations and not revisit it soon.

You've been asked to lead this decision. What you recommend this afternoon matters more than it might look like from the outside.

A quick word on where ideas come from

Every pitch on your desk this morning came from somewhere different — and where an idea comes from shapes how much people trust it, how fast it moves, and what it's missing.

You'll see each pitch tagged with one of four sources:

  • INTERNALFrom staff or managers already inside Harborline.
  • EXTERNAL / EXPERTFrom consultants, industry reports, or benchmarking data.
  • CUSTOMER / COMMUNITYFrom surveys, direct feedback, or the people Harborline actually serves.
  • OPEN INNOVATIONFrom unconventional, informal channels — like a suggestion box — that don't come through normal reporting lines.

None of these sources is automatically "better." Each comes with its own blind spots. Keep half an eye on where your picks are coming from as you go — you'll be asked about it later.
